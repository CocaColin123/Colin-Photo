# Colin Photography

## Register

brand

## Product Purpose

Colin Photography is a personal photography site shaped like a small authored world. The site should let visitors enter through a cinematic cover, browse curated photo chapters, open images in an immersive viewer, read short diary entries, and understand the photographer's way of seeing without turning the experience into a resume or a generic portfolio.

## Audience

- Friends, collaborators, and visitors who want to experience Colin's photography as a personal body of work.
- People who arrive from social links and need a clear way to view albums, read selected writing, and contact Colin.
- Future viewers who may follow updates through diary posts rather than formal blog articles.

## Brand Voice

Quiet, observational, and intimate. The site should feel like a night walk through a personal archive: water, trees, animals, city edges, light, and small moments. Copy should be short and precise. Avoid promotional language, metrics, marketing claims, and long explanatory blocks.

## Core Experience

- Landing: a cover moment that establishes mood and invites entry.
- Works: a curated directory of photo chapters, with images as the primary surface.
- Album: a chapter page with a strong cover image, concise context, rhythmic photo layout, and immersive lightbox.
- About: a restrained author page about how Colin sees, not a conventional CV.
- Journal: a lightweight inner section for diary entries. It should support personal writing, dates, optional image pairing, and calm reading, without becoming a heavy blog platform.

## Design Principles

- Photography is the main character. UI effects must support image viewing rather than compete with it.
- Keep the "Colin's world" ritual, but make it feel authored and photographic rather than purely game-like.
- Use darkness as a viewing room, not as decoration. Contrast must preserve legibility and photo detail.
- Preserve Chinese typography and poetic album language, but avoid overloading every image with text.
- Interactions should feel tactile and quiet: hover, keyboard, swipe, and light motion are welcome when they reduce friction.

## Anti-References

- Generic portfolio grids with identical cards.
- Heavy glassmorphism, ornamental gradients, and decorative effects that obscure photos.
- Blog-first layouts that make diary writing feel like a content marketing site.
- Gear-focused photography pages.
- Long About pages that read like a resume.

## Success Criteria

- A visitor understands within a few seconds that this is a personal photography world.
- The path from landing to works to album to lightbox feels deliberate and low-friction.
- The site has enough structure for About and Journal without adding unnecessary navigation complexity.
- The visual system remains distinctive, but photos remain easier to inspect than the UI effects.
